Losses and regularization

What a network is trained to minimize. First the loss itself: softmax, cross-entropy and KL, and why cross-entropy beats squared error, as in homework 1. Then the regularizers, which all change that objective: a penalty on the weights, noise injected while training, the noise of the minibatch itself, and the capacity of the network.

  1. Softmax

    Softmax

    Drag the logits and watch the probabilities, and the Jacobian ∂pi/∂zj as a live matrix (homework 1 question 1.3.1). Adding a constant to every logit changes nothing, each row of the Jacobian sums to zero, it all flattens when one class dominates, and a temperature slides softmax between argmax and uniform.

  3. Why cross-entropy

    Squared error vs. cross-entropy

    Why classifiers train better with cross-entropy than with the quadratic cost, as Glorot & Bengio remark, and homework 1 question 1.3.4 asks. Both are negative log-likelihoods (squared error is a Gaussian’s, and a KL between Gaussians); the difference is that squared error’s gradient keeps the sigmoid’s slope and vanishes when the output is confidently wrong, while cross-entropy’s log cancels it.

  4. Weight decay

    Weight decay: L2 against L1

    Homework 2.1’s penalty makes every gradient step a shrink first. Read as a budget, L2 is a ball and L1 a diamond whose corners zero out weights. A penalty turns a wildly bumpy twelve-point fit smooth, and inside Adam the same penalty is not weight decay, which is why AdamW exists.

  7. Gradient noise

    Batch size, learning rate, and gradient noise

    A minibatch gradient is the full gradient plus noise that shrinks like 1/√B, and vanishes at B = N (HW2.1’s full batch). SGD settles into a cloud whose size is set by lr/B, which is why the linear scaling rule works, until lr passes 2/λmax. And enough noise carries SGD out of a sharp minimum into a wide one.
