Bad init, watched training: the failure, not just the theory

A real 6-hidden-layer tanh network training with plain gradient descent on two interleaved moons. Pick an init scale and press Train: watch the decision boundary, the loss curve, and the network itself, and each hidden layer's activations and gradients as histograms, live.

init σ 1.00 depth 6
Full-batch gradient descent, fixed learning rate, width 10, tanh hidden units, sigmoid output. Every weight starts as a Gaussian sample from N(0, σ²) and every bias at 0; the Xavier button sets σ = √(2/(10+10)) ≈ 0.32, Glorot's value for a 10→10 layer; the slider starts at the naïve σ = 1. Train runs 400 steps; press it again to keep going for another 400. Network: one line per weight (orange positive, blue negative, thickness and opacity ∝ |w|), one node per neuron colored by its activation on the probe point (hover the boundary plot to move it), ringed red when saturated. Activations: every hidden unit's activation over all 120 training points, one panel per layer, red where the unit is saturated (|a|>0.995, so tanh′<0.01) and green where it still passes gradient. Gradients: the backprop gradient ∂L/∂a at every hidden unit for every training point, binned by log₁₀ of its magnitude on one shared axis from 10⁻⁶ to about 1 (ticks at 10⁻⁴, 10⁻², and a taller one at 1); red bars at the ends collect everything smaller than 10⁻⁶ (vanished) or larger than about 3 (exploding). Gradient flows right to left, so vanishing shows as mass sliding left toward L1. Too-small init: the weights are nearly invisible, activations collapse to a spike at 0, gradients shrink by orders of magnitude per layer, and loss barely moves. Too-big init: thick weights, every unit pinned at ±1, and the boundary stays close to its random start.
