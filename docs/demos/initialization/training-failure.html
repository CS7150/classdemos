Bad init, watched training: the failure, not just the theory

A real 6-hidden-layer tanh network training with plain gradient descent on two interleaved moons. Pick an init scale and press Train: watch the decision boundary, the loss curve, and each hidden layer's activations — as histograms, or as the network itself — live.

scale ×Xavier 1.0× depth 6
Full-batch gradient descent, fixed learning rate, width 10, tanh hidden units, sigmoid output. Train runs 400 steps; press it again to keep going for another 400. Histograms: every hidden unit's activation over all 120 training points, one panel per layer, red where the unit is saturated (|a|>0.975, so tanh′<0.05) and green where it still passes gradient. Network: one line per weight (orange positive, blue negative, thickness and opacity ∝ |w|), one node per neuron colored by its activation on the probe point (hover the boundary plot to move it), ringed red when saturated. Too-small init: the weights are nearly invisible, activations collapse to a spike at 0, and loss barely moves. Too-big init: thick weights, every unit pinned at ±1, and the boundary stays close to its random start.
