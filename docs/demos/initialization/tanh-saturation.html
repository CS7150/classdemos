Saturation on one tanh: every unit's slope, at a glance

Twenty units, each sitting on the tanh curve at its own pre-activation h, each drawn with its tangent line: the slope tanh′(h) is how much gradient that unit passes back. Scale the inputs up and the units slide out onto the flat tails; scale them down and they huddle at 0.

input scale: h ~ N(0, s²), s 1.00
Each unit's pre-activation is h = s·z for a fixed standard normal z, so the slider stretches the same twenty units apart or squeezes them together; in a network, s is what the weight scale controls (where the n comes from shows how n·σw² sets it). Every tangent is drawn the same length, so its angle is its slope: tanh′(h) = 1 − tanh²(h), which is 1 at h = 0 and below 0.01 once |h| > 3. Big inputs: the outputs are pinned near ±1 and still carry a signal, but the slopes are near 0, so almost no gradient gets back through, which is saturation. Small inputs: every slope is nearly 1, so tanh passes gradients through untouched, but the outputs all huddle near 0, a signal that has all but vanished. The h axis is fixed at −5 to 5; units beyond it are pinned to the edge and counted. The chain-rule demo shows what these slopes do once they multiply through several layers.
