Forward vs. backward: Glorot's compromise

One layer, n_in inputs and n_out outputs. Keeping the forward signal's variance stable wants Var(W)=1/n_in; keeping the backward gradient's variance stable wants Var(W)=1/n_out. When the layer isn't square, nothing satisfies both — Glorot's formula picks the variance that minimizes how badly either one drifts.

Each group of bars is one scheme's Var(W); the two bars show the resulting forward factor n_in·Var(W) and backward factor n_out·Var(W) (1.0 = perfectly preserved, the dashed line). Forward-only Xavier nails the forward bar but the backward bar drifts by the layer's aspect ratio, and vice versa for backward-only; Glorot's harmonic-ish average keeps both within a factor of 2 of 1 for any ratio.
