Weight scale and saturation: where the gradient goes

1000 units, each fed a pre-activation h = W·a from a width-48 layer with input a ~ N(0,1). Dots along the curve show where those units land; the shaded band is the local gradient φ′(h) backprop has to multiply through.

weight scale, × Xavier 1.00×
Xavier sets Var(W) = 1/n_in so h lands with unit variance — squarely in tanh's near-linear middle, where the average gradient is close to 1. Scale weights down and every unit sits near h=0 too, but the *signal itself* shrinks layer to layer (see the depth demo). Scale them up and units pile into the flat tails, where φ′(h)≈0 kills the backward gradient even though the forward signal survives.
