Standard vs. normalized init: two uniform boxes

Both of Glorot & Bengio's initializations draw each weight uniformly from an interval [−a, a]; they differ only in the half-width a. The standard one (common before 2010) uses a = 1/√nin; their normalized one uses a = √6 / √(nin + nout). Everything follows from one fact: a uniform on [−a, a] has variance a²/3.

Why a uniform on [−a, a] has variance a²/3

The variance is the average of w² over the interval. Its own page shows why that average is a third of the biggest square: stack the squares and they make a pyramid, and three such pyramids fill a cube. The right-hand panels below show the same fact as Archimedes did: the area under the parabola y = w² is 1/3 of its bounding box, so its mean height is a²/3.

Where the standard init's 1/3 comes from

The standard init uses 1/√n as the uniform's half-width, not its standard deviation. A uniform's standard deviation is only a/√3 ≈ 0.58a of its half-width, so the weights come out √3 too narrow and n·Var(W) = 1/3. Had 1/√n been the standard deviation (say, of a Gaussian, as LeCun et al. recommended in 1998), n·Var(W) would be 1 and the 1/3 would be gone. It is also where the √6 in the normalized init comes from: to reach a variance of 2/(nin + nout), the half-width must be √3 times that standard deviation, and √3·√2 = √6.

Histogram: flat, because every value in [−a, a] is equally likely. With nin = nout = n, the normalized box is √3 times wider than the standard one, giving variance 1/n instead of 1/(3n). That factor of 3 is the per-layer shrinkage the variance page shows.
