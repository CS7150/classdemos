Unrolling the EMA: why the recurrence is a geometric series

Cut a strip of length 1 into pieces of size (1−β), (1−β)β, (1−β)β², … — the classic geometric-series picture. Those exact pieces are the weights the EMA recurrence puts on each past sample, most recent first.

Revealing term k cuts a (1−β)βk fraction off the strip's remaining βk, and weights xt−k (k steps before the present) by that same fraction in the data below. Cut every term and the weights sum to exactly 1 and the weighted sum exactly equals the recurrence — stop early (as any running computation must) and both fall short by precisely βk, which is exactly what dividing by (1−βk) corrects for.
