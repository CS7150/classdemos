EMA and bias correction: the machinery inside Adam

The raw EMA* starts at zero no matter what the data is, so a constant signal is dramatically underestimated at first. The bias-corrected EMA fixes that instantly — then behaves like an ordinary (lagging) moving average once the data itself changes.

Avg←Avg+(x−Avg)/t
EMA*←βEMA*+(1−β)x
EMA←EMA*/(1−βt)
β 0.90
Grey dots = raw data xt. The equal-forever average weighs every past sample the same, so after the step's jump it drags along very slowly — the EMA (with its decay) adapts much faster, at the cost of needing bias correction early on.
