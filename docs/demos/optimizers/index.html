Optimizers, explained

Six interactive pieces building up to Adam: how momentum averages out noisy gradients, the exponential moving average and bias correction that make that averaging precise, the trouble a single learning rate has with an anisotropic bowl, the mean/RMS diagnostic Adam adds on top, why its update is scale-invariant, and how network design (activations, initialization, depth) fights the same conditioning problem from the other side.

  1. Momentum

    Momentum averages out noise

    A smooth bowl with a high-frequency ripple on top. Plain gradient descent gets stuck bouncing inside one ripple; momentum's exponential moving average of the gradient cancels the alternating ripple and rides the bowl down.

    Open the explainer →
  2. EMA and bias correction

    EMA and bias correction

    Raw data against three averages overlaid — the raw exponential moving average, the bias-corrected version, and a plain equal-weighted average forever — each with its own show/hide checkbox, so you can see exactly what the correction buys you.

    Open the explainer →
  3. Gradient descent

    Anisotropic gradient descent

    A bowl that's steep in one direction and shallow in another. Adjust the anisotropy, watch a single learning rate zig-zag or crawl, then switch to per-axis rates and see the difference — in a 1D per-axis slice, a 2D contour map, or a 3D surface.

    Open the explainer →
  4. The Adam diagnostic

    Mean vs. RMS

    A synthetic gradient stream with adjustable drift, sign-flip frequency, and noise, and the running mean and RMS drawn live — the exact gap that tells Adam when to slow down.

    Open the explainer →
  5. Why Adam is scale-invariant

    Adam vs. SGD, rescaled

    The same descent run side by side under SGD and Adam while a slider multiplies every gradient by K. SGD's steps grow with K; Adam's barely change.

    Open the explainer →

    Depth, activation, and initialization

    Watch a signal spread through a network of adjustable depth, and see which combinations of activation and initialization keep it alive versus vanish or blow up.
