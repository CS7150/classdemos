Reinforcement learning for language models

How a language model is trained on a reward instead of on text to imitate. The REINFORCE and DPO pieces use one model small enough to see whole: four tokens, three steps, 64 possible completions, with every next-token distribution on screen. Everything trains live in the browser, and because the model is so small, the quantities a real run can only estimate (expected reward, KL, the optimal policy itself) are computed exactly and drawn alongside. Two shorter pieces, on a line of responses and a line of scores, explain the optimum they aim at and the model of human preferences behind DPO.

  1. Policy gradients

    From REINFORCE to GRPO

    REINFORCE as fine-tuning on the model’s own samples, weighted by reward; a baseline that leaves the gradient unbiased and cuts its noise; and GRPO, which uses the group of samples as its own baseline. All three train side by side toward the KL-regularized optimum, drawn as a dashed target. Add a constant to every reward to see plain REINFORCE lose its way, and find the groups that teach GRPO nothing.

  2. The optimum

    The tilted distribution

    Maximizing reward minus β times a KL penalty has a closed form: the reference model multiplied by ereward/β and renormalized. On a one-dimensional space of responses, slide β from “stay put” to “collapse onto the reward”, drag two responses to see their odds change by exactly egap/β with the normalizer cancelling, view it in log scale where reward is simply added, and watch reward hacking appear as the KL budget grows.

  3. Preferences as probabilities

    Bradley-Terry: from a hidden score to a preference

    A person prefers A with probability σ(rA−rB). Why only score gaps matter (add a constant to both and nothing changes), where the sigmoid comes from (noisy judgments, with Gumbel noise giving it exactly), and how a reward model recovers the scores from votes by logistic regression.

  4. Learning from preferences

    DPO: the same target, from a fixed set of preferences

    Invert the optimum and the policy becomes a reward model, which turns preference learning into logistic regression on pairs. DPO and GRPO race to the same target: watch which completions the pairs pin down, the implicit reward each one ends up with, and DPO overfitting its pairs until more data or online resampling rescues it.
