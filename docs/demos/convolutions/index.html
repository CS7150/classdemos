Convolutions, explained

Interactive pieces for the convolution lecture and LeCun et al. 1989: first, why comparing images pixel by pixel fails as soon as something moves; then the oriented detector Hubel and Wiesel found in the cat's visual cortex, rebuilt as a small grid of weights, and why you need a copy of it at every position.

  1. The problem

    Shift breaks pixel distance

    A digit, the same digit moved a few pixels, and a different digit. Drag the copy and watch its distance from the original climb past the distance to other digits, often after a single pixel, until a nearest-neighbour classifier on pixels calls it something else. Blurring first only postpones it.

  2. Hubel and Wiesel

    Orientation tuning

    Turn a bar of light in front of one detector, the lecture’s −½ +1 −½ edge-finder rotated, and trace its tuning curve while it crackles. Move the bar off centre and it goes silent, which is why the same detector has to be copied to every position; a bank of eight angles reads out the orientation.
