Quiz 0, worked

Quiz 0 is not a test. It exists to tell me what this particular class already knows, so that I teach the right things. That is why the useful answer to a question you cannot do is “don’t know” rather than a guess.

Here is an interactive explainer for each technical question on it. Every one runs in the browser, and every one lets you change the numbers: the point is not the answer to the particular question, it is the picture behind it.

  1. Linear algebra

    Matrix × vector

    Multiply by hand. [1 2 −1; 0 1 0] [3; 4; 5] =

    The algebra and the geometry, running side by side: x turns on its side and deals one entry to each column, A comes apart into columns, every column is stretched by its own coefficient at the same moment, and the stretched columns fly into a chain whose end is Ax. Edit any number, or change the shape of A, and watch your own product build itself.

  2. Linear algebra

    Least squares

    What quantity does the least-squares solution minimize? You want to fit y ≈ Ax where A is 100×3.

    Drag a fit and watch the squared residuals as literal squares, then the same thing as geometry: y projected onto the column space, with the error perpendicular to everything the model can reach.

    Open the explainer →
  3. Calculus

    The chain rule

    Write dz/dw. Let u = wx + b with x and b fixed, and z = g(u).

    A nudge in w travelling down the chain, with a magnifier at each link showing the stretch factor it picks up. Derivatives multiply because stretches compose.

    Open the explainer →
  4. Probability

    Expectation and variance

    E[X] and Var(X) for X equal to 1 with probability p and 0 otherwise.

    The mean as a balance point, the variance as the average squared distance from it, and why the spread is widest at p = ½ and vanishes at either end.

    Open the explainer →
  5. Probability

    Conditional probability

    P(disease | positive)? 1% of people have it; the test is positive for 99% of them and for 5% of everyone else.

    Ten thousand people as ten thousand squares. The positives fly out and re-sort, and the answer is the fraction of that group who are actually sick.

    Open the explainer →
  6. Statistical learning

    Capacity

    Does making the model more flexible increase or decrease bias, variance, and training error?

    One true curve, one noisy sample, and a flexibility dial. Resample and the fits scatter: the scatter is variance, the offset is bias, and training error slides to zero whatever else happens.
